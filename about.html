About

We are an experimental research group working at the interface of atomic, molecular, and optical (AMO) and condensed matter physics. We build superconducting quantum circuits to study open quantum systems: quantum systems that interact with their environment, and whose measurement, dissipation, and control we harness to probe fundamental questions in quantum mechanics.

Non-Hermitian Quantum Systems

Our group explores the physics of open quantum systems whose dynamics are non-Hermitian: systems with engineered gain, loss, and dissipation that fall outside the usual closed-system picture of quantum mechanics. Using superconducting qubits and microwave circuits, we realize effective non-Hermitian dynamics through continuous monitoring and controlled dissipation, and study the striking phenomena that emerge near exceptional points, where the topology of the quantum state space itself changes character as it winds around these degeneracies. We track how quantum jumps interrupt and reshape this evolution, measure the thermodynamic work fluctuations that accompany it, and probe surprising breakdowns of the ordinary rules of quantum superposition along the way. Beyond their fundamental interest, these effects point toward new tools for quantum sensing, amplification, and information processing, and toward a richer understanding of how measurement and dissipation shape quantum evolution.

Quantum Sensing and Quantum Metrology

Our group investigates the fundamental limits of measurement precision using superconducting qubits as a controllable quantum sensor platform. Grounded in the framework of quantum metrology — where the quantum Cramér-Rao bound, set by the quantum Fisher information, defines the ultimate precision achievable by any unbiased estimator — our experiments are designed to probe and saturate these limits. We have demonstrated agnostic phase estimation that achieves near-optimal sensitivity without prior knowledge of the signal, broadening the practical reach of quantum-enhanced measurement. We further showed that superconducting antiqubits, realized via unitary inversion of the qubit's evolution, enable optimal phase estimation that saturates the Cramér-Rao bound at Heisenberg-limited sensitivity. Building on this, we are currently extending our platform to multiparameter estimation, where simultaneously sensing multiple unknowns introduces fundamental measurement incompatibilities and tradeoffs that go beyond the single-parameter case.

Many-body quantum dynamics

Interacting quantum systems can exhibit collective phenomena that have no counterpart at the level of individual particles, including long-range entanglement, spontaneous symmetry breaking, and topological order. While these concepts are well established for isolated systems, coupling to an environment can qualitatively reshape the nature of quantum order rather than simply destroy it. Using programmable arrays of superconducting qubits, we study the preparation, stabilization, and dynamics of entangled many-body states in open quantum systems. Our work explores how symmetry, entanglement, and collective order evolve under decoherence and engineered dissipation, and how new phases and dynamical phenomena can emerge far from equilibrium. These experiments provide a platform for investigating and controlling novel forms of quantum matter using coherent interactions, dissipation, and measurement.

Quantum measurement and trajectories

Quantum measurement does more than reveal the state of a quantum system: it also changes its subsequent evolution. When measurements are performed continuously or weakly, this backaction unfolds gradually, producing stochastic quantum trajectories that can be reconstructed from the measurement record. We study how information acquired through measurement can be used as a resource for controlling quantum dynamics. By developing adaptive measurement protocols and real-time feedback, we use measurement records to guide the evolution of quantum systems toward desired states and trajectories. This framework enables new approaches to initialization, reset, stabilization, and trajectory control, while providing a direct window into the interplay between information, backaction, and stochastic quantum dynamics.

Quantum coherence engineering

Highly coherent qubits are the backbone of quantum technology. We constantly explore novel approaches to designing and fabricating superconducting-circuit-based devices. Recently, we directed our device expertise to a novel hybrid quantum platform based on single electrons floating on solid neon films that are coated on top of superconducting circuits. Leveraging our knowledge of superconducting circuits, we revealed that the substrate underlying the neon film dominates the behavior of the observed electron charge states. We are currently using this platform to study the origin of two-level systems on the silicon surface, which are thought to lead to decoherence in superconducting circuits.

Device-Based Probes of Quantum Materials

Just like how discoveries of new materials lead to new technologies, new technologies can also assist with the discovery of new materials. Our lab uses its expertise in microwave circuitry and nanofabrication to probe novel quantum materials. We use microwave cavity perturbation to study the electromagnetic response of unconventional superconductors through changes in cavity resonance frequency and loss. We also develop microfabricated circuits for diamond anvil cells, enabling electrical transport measurements under extreme pressure. In parallel, our work on YBCO coplanar waveguide resonators explores the microwave properties of high-temperature superconductors and their potential for sensing applications.
